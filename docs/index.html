Bluetooth in your Proxmox Linux VMs. Finally.

Pair a controller, headphones, or a Home Assistant sensor inside a VM - even with a Bluetooth chip that "can't be passed through."

TL;DR: Bluetooth passthrough to a Proxmox VM fails for Intel onboard chips (BE200, AX210, AX211 - by hardware design, no setting fixes it) and is unreliable on gaming distros like ChimeraOS and Bazzite. This tool shares the host's Bluetooth with the VM over the network instead. Two commands. Works with any chip Linux supports. Survives reboots.

Two commands

On the Proxmox host:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lucid-fabrics/proxmox-bluetooth/main/install.sh | sudo bash

It prints one line - paste that inside your VM. Done.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I just pass the USB Bluetooth controller through to the VM?

Intel CNVi Bluetooth (the BE200/AX2xx family found on most modern motherboards) requires the host's own driver to load its firmware at boot. Any passthrough handoff - USB redirect, vfio, driver unbind - resets the chip to its ROM bootloader, and the guest can never complete the firmware handshake. It's not a bug you can patch around; it's how the chip is designed. Plain USB dongles into plain Linux guests often do work - try that first.

Will my controller lag?

No. The bridge adds well under a millisecond on the same machine. Bluetooth's own radio latency is slower than that.

Does it work for headphones, keyboards, or Home Assistant sensors?

Yes. The bridge is protocol-transparent - it forwards raw Bluetooth traffic without understanding or filtering it. Anything that works on a normal Linux Bluetooth adapter works: controllers, audio, HID, and BLE sensors for Home Assistant.

Do I need to buy new hardware?

No. The Bluetooth chip you already have works - even the one you might have already replaced trying to fix this.

Does it survive ChimeraOS or Bazzite OS updates?

Yes. Those distros replace the system image on update but keep /etc and /var, which is exactly where this installs. The bridge and your pairings come back on their own.

Is this Proxmox-specific?

No - any Linux host running KVM VMs works, or even two separate physical machines. Proxmox is just where this problem is reported the most.
